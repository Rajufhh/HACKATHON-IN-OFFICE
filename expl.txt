1. First, understand your project
Project name: Academic Integrity & Similarity Case Management System

In simple words:

Imagine a college where thousands of students submit assignments.

Sometimes students copy content from websites, other students or existing documents.

Your system helps the college:
- Check how similar student assignments are.
- Identify assignments that need investigation.
- Assign teachers or reviewers to investigate.
- Store proof and evidence.
- Record the final decision.
- Allow students to appeal if they disagree.
The most important point: Your system does not automatically punish students based on similarity percentage. A human reviewer investigates before making a decision.

5. What is special about your project?
An interviewer may ask: "What makes your project different?"

You can explain three important features.

1. Reviewer conflict checking

Suppose a teacher has a personal connection with a student.

Your system checks conflicts before assigning that teacher to the case.

2. Evidence history

Suppose a reviewer makes a decision today and someone adds new evidence tomorrow.

Your system should preserve the original decision and its evidence snapshot. It should not silently change the old record.

3. Independent appeal

Suppose Teacher A made the original decision.

The appeal should be reviewed by another eligible reviewer, not Teacher A.

These features help make the system fair and transparent.

7. Interview answer — very short version
If the interviewer asks, "Explain your project," say this:
My project is an Academic Integrity and Similarity Case Management System.

The main purpose of this project is to help colleges manage student assignment similarity checks and academic misconduct investigations.

When a student submits an assignment, the system generates a similarity report. If the similarity score crosses a configured threshold, the system creates a flag for human review.

An administrator can create an investigation case and assign a reviewer. The reviewer checks the evidence and records a decision with a proper explanation.

If the student disagrees with the decision, they can submit an appeal, which must be reviewed independently.

I developed the backend using Python and FastAPI, the frontend using React and TypeScript, and I used MySQL with SQLAlchemy for database management.

I also designed a Python synthetic data generator to create large amounts of test data and validate relationships, business rules and exceptional scenarios.

The main focus of my project is fairness, evidence management, traceability and proper human decision-making.





























My project uses a relational database architecture with MySQL.

I divided the database into multiple related tables, such as Learner, Assessment, Submission, Similarity Report, Case, Reviewer, Evidence, Decision and Appeal.

I used primary keys and foreign keys to maintain relationships between tables and avoid duplicate data.

For example, one learner can have multiple submissions, and each submission can have similarity reports.

I also used separate tables to manage case assignments, evidence, decisions and appeals.

One important feature is maintaining event history so that important changes are traceable instead of simply overwriting previous records.

This database design helps maintain data consistency, transparency and proper investigation workflow.
